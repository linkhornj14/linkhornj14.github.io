This paper examines how state reductions in unemployment insurance (UI) benefit duration reshape the distribution of non-employment and the quality of re-employment for displaced workers. Between 2020 and 2025, ten U.S. states cut their maximum weeks of regular UI below the 26-week standard. Because a duration cut moves the benefit cap, its effect operates precisely at and beyond that cap; restricting attention to short (≤26-week) spells therefore conditions on a treatment-affected outcome and discards the welfare-relevant tail. This paper instead estimates the effect of the cuts on the full duration distribution. The cut states followed a distinctively flat post-COVID duration path, so no cross-state control group satisfies parallel trends. Identification is therefore moved within state, comparing more- and less-exposed workers so that state×time fixed effects absorb each state's pandemic trajectory, using Current Population Survey (CPS) microdata and administrative worker-level data from the Longitudinal Employer-Household Dynamics (LEHD).

*Worker-level companion to "The Effects of State-Level Unemployment Benefit Reductions on Labor Market Outcomes."*
