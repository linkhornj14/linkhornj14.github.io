This paper examines whether households use online crowdfunding platforms as informal insurance against adverse economic shocks. I find that access to federal unemployment benefits during high unemployment leads to reduced campaign creation on GoFundMe, suggesting that crowdfunding serves a genuine consumption-smoothing tool — particularly for households with limited access to formal insurance.

*Presented at the Southern Economic Association 94th Annual Meeting (2024).*
