This paper investigates the relationship between federal disaster assistance and private charitable giving through online crowdfunding platforms. A large body of economic theory suggests that government provision of a public good should "crowd out" private contributions. However, the immediate aftermath of natural disasters is often characterized by a massive outpouring of private donations, even when a substantial federal response is anticipated. I test for the presence of crowding out while also exploring the hypothesis that "warm glow" or expressive giving, driven by the immediacy of the event, plays a significant role. Using quasi-experimental methods, including an instrumental variable approach that leverages the political economy of aid distribution, I estimate the causal effect of FEMA aid on crowdfunding. This research contributes to the literature on public finance and the economics of charity, with direct policy implications for disaster relief financing.
